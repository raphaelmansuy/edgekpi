A SQL query is not a KPI

Why a parameterised query is the wrong place to define a business metric, and what to use instead.

By Raphael MANSUY

A common pattern stores each engagement KPI as a SQL query with parameters: a date range, some filters and a slot for the grouping. That is enough for one number in one table. It is not enough when the business asks for the same KPIs by typology, tier and campaign, across years and markets, with subtotals and grand totals. A query records how to produce an answer. It does not record what the answer means, so the answer cannot be safely regrouped.

The problem

Totals you cannot trust, layouts you cannot change

Subtotals of rates and of unique audiences come out wrong when they are built from the figures on screen. Every new layout needs new SQL.

The cause

The query keeps the result and drops the ingredients

It returns finished numbers at one grouping. What is needed to regroup them is gone: the numerator, the denominator, and who was counted.

The solution

Define each KPI once, with no grouping inside

Store the ingredients and the formula. Let the reader choose rows, columns and totals. Compute every cell and every total from its own data.

A SQL query is not a KPIPart 1 · The problem

Five reasons a parameterised query falls short

A parameterised query is SQL text with blanks: values for dates and filters, and a list of columns to group by. Filling the blanks runs the query and returns one finished number per group. The weaknesses below come from that design, not from anyone's SQL.

1

It stores an answer, not a meaning

The query returns 90%. The 81 delivered and 90 sent messages behind it are not returned, so it cannot be combined with another figure.

Business impact

Rates of 90% and 50% on 90 and 10 messages average to 70%; the answer is 86%.

2

The grouping is written into the text

A query computes at one level of detail. Another level means editing the text or pasting a new column list into it, which is text substitution and not a safe value parameter.

Business impact

Each new layout is a change request, with a fresh chance of error each time.

3

It cannot say which kind of number it returns

Messages sent add up. A rate must be recomputed. Unique clients must be recounted, because one person can be in two campaigns. All three look the same coming out of a query.

Business impact

The report guesses the total rule: 6 distinct clients were reached, yet two market cells add up to 9.

4

Every KPI works alone

Six KPIs at twelve combinations of detail, subtotal and grand total would be 72 separate executions in a straightforward design, although they read the same messages and share ingredients.

Business impact

Run time and warehouse cost grow with every KPI and every subtotal added.

5

The business definition stays hidden

Owner, population, time basis and what counts as "reached" exist only inside SQL. Does Reach count messages or people? The query gives one answer and does not say which.

Business impact

Owners cannot approve a definition they cannot read, and two reports can disagree unnoticed.

What parameterised queries do well

They bind dates and filter values safely, and they are a sound way to run a calculation. For a fixed report with one number per row they are adequate. The limits appear when the same KPI must be read at several levels at once. SQL stays the engine; it should stop being the definition.

Define the KPI once, then ask for any view

Separate what the business owns from what the reader chooses. The business defines measures (what is counted) and formulas (how measures combine into a KPI), with no grouping in either. The reader chooses the layout. An engine computes every cell and total from its own messages.

Databricks metric views

Preferred

A metric view is an object in the data catalogue that stores measures and the formulas built on them. Reports read it with ordinary SQL plus one function, MEASURE(), and choose the grouping in the query. It is preferred where the platform is Databricks, because the definition is governed alongside the data.

1

Define once

No grouping in the definition
CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW crm.gold.engagement_kpi
WITH METRICS LANGUAGE YAML AS $$
version: 1.1
comment: "Engagement KPIs, one row per message sent"
source: crm.silver.message
dimensions:
  - name: typology
    expr: typology
  - name: market
    expr: market
measures:
  - name: sent
    expr: COUNT(*)
  - name: delivered
    expr: COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE is_delivered)
  - name: unique_clients
    expr: COUNT(DISTINCT client_id)
  - name: delivery_rate
    expr: MEASURE(delivered) * 1.0 / NULLIF(MEASURE(sent), 0)
    comment: "Delivered messages divided by sent messages"
$$;
2

Ask for a view

Reader's choice
SELECT typology, market,
       GROUPING_ID(typology, market) AS gid,
       MEASURE(sent)           AS sent,
       MEASURE(delivery_rate)  AS delivery_rate,
       MEASURE(unique_clients) AS unique_clients
FROM crm.gold.engagement_kpi
GROUP BY GROUPING SETS (
  (typology, market),   -- detail cells
  (typology),           -- typology totals
  (market),             -- market totals
  ()                    -- grand total
);
3

Read the result

One row per group
gidThe row isRows here
0a detail cell4
1a typology total, all markets2
2a market total, all typologies2
3the grand total1

How it works

  1. The view lists what may be grouped by (dimensions) and what is counted (measures). It contains no GROUP BY.
  2. A rate is written from other measures. The engine evaluates numerator and denominator at whatever grouping the reader asks for, then divides.
  3. GROUPING SETS requests detail, subtotals and grand total together. gid tells a total from a genuine "unknown" value.

Strengths

  • One definition in Unity Catalog, with the permissions and lineage of the data.
  • Consumers keep writing SQL. Nothing extra to run.
  • The comment puts the business meaning next to the formula.

Watch for

  • Write the NULLIF guard in every rate. In its default mode Databricks raises an error on division by zero.
  • Exact unique counts at many levels can be slow. Measure before committing.
  • Needs a recent runtime: 16.4 or later to create a metric view, per Databricks documentation.

Tested

A version with five dimensions and twelve grouping sets ran on a Databricks serverless SQL warehouse on 12 November 2026 and matched all 56 groups. The two-dimension form on this page is that statement reduced; it has not been run in this form.

Still to prove before production

Report filters, row-level access rules under the reader's identity, empty and unknown groups, and cost at real volumes. These are the remaining acceptance tests.

Malloy

Alternative

Malloy is an open-source language for describing data. A source declares measures once. A query nests one level of the report inside another. Malloy compiles the whole request into a single SQL statement and returns a tree shaped like the report.

1

Define once

No grouping in the definition
source: message is duckdb.table('message') extend {
  measure:
    sent is count()
    delivered is count() { where: is_delivered }
    unique_clients is count(client_id)
    delivery_rate is delivered / nullif(sent, 0)
}

2

Ask for a view

One nest per report level
query: pivot is message -> {
  // grand total
  aggregate: sent, delivery_rate, unique_clients

  // market totals
  nest: by_market is {
    group_by: market
    aggregate: sent, delivery_rate, unique_clients
  }

  // typology totals, with their detail cells inside
  nest: by_typology is {
    group_by: typology
    aggregate: sent, delivery_rate, unique_clients
    nest: by_market is {
      group_by: market
      aggregate: sent, delivery_rate, unique_clients
    }
  }
}

How it works

  1. measure: names a calculation with no grouping. A rate is written from two other measures.
  2. group_by with aggregate evaluates the measures at one level. nest: puts a finer level inside it.
  3. Each level is computed from its own rows. Malloy writes one SQL statement that repeats each row once per level; it does not use GROUPING SETS.

Strengths

  • The same model runs on several engines: connectors exist for DuckDB, BigQuery, Snowflake, Postgres, Trino and Databricks.
  • The result has the shape of the report's row hierarchy.
  • Open source, and the model is a text file under version control.

Watch for

  • No automatic zero guard: an unguarded rate returned NaN. Write nullif in every rate.
  • A column axis means repeating a nest at each row level, and the pivot code must flatten the tree.
  • A second language and a runtime to operate. Packages are at version 0.0.x.

Tested

This model and query ran on DuckDB with Malloy 0.0.437 and returned the nine groups above. The five-dimension version matched all 56 groups, also on DuckDB. Nothing was run on Databricks.

Choose it when

The same definitions must run on more than one database engine, or a nested result is what the consumer needs. Then run the same acceptance tests through its Databricks connector first.

For gaps

Keep the definitions in your own catalogue and let a small planner write the SQL for each request. It gives the most control and the most to maintain, so use it where the native option falls short.

What the planner generates

Produced by the prototype planner · Executed
WITH agg AS (
  SELECT typology, market,
    GROUPING_ID(typology, market) AS gid,
    COUNT(*) AS sent,
    COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE is_delivered) AS delivered,
    COUNT(DISTINCT client_id) AS unique_clients
  FROM message
  WHERE TRUE
  GROUP BY GROUPING SETS ((typology, market), (typology), (market), ())
  HAVING COUNT(*) > 0
)
SELECT typology, market, gid, sent, delivered, unique_clients, delivered * 1.0 / NULLIF(sent, 0) AS delivery_rate
FROM agg

How it works

  1. The planner checks every requested name against the catalogue and refuses unknown ones.
  2. It writes each needed measure once in the inner block, with one grouping set per report level.
  3. Formulas run outside, after aggregation. ratio() always adds the zero guard, and HAVING drops empty groups.

Strengths

  • Full control of the plan: a heavy unique count can run in its own statement.
  • Works on any SQL engine.
  • Guards, validation and result checks live in one place.

Watch for

  • You own the planner: validation, security, totals and tests.
  • Names come from the catalogue and values are bound as parameters, never pasted into text.
  • Avoid two definitions of one KPI if metric views exist too.

Tested

Generated by the prototype planner and run on DuckDB: the nine expected groups. The five-dimension version matched all 56 groups on DuckDB and on a Databricks warehouse. The catalogue and request formats are proposals.

Choose it when

An acceptance test shows a gap in the native option, such as a total it cannot express or a unique count that is too slow. Build that capability only.

Choosing between the three

All three keep the KPI definition free of grouping and compute each total from its own data, so they return the same numbers. Migrate by inferring the definitions

A registry table typically holds every KPI as a SQL template with a grouping placeholder such as {{ group_by_list }}. Each template already contains the measures and the expression of its KPI; they are simply not separated. A tool can read the template, infer both, and prove that the result gives the same numbers. A person then names the measures and confirms the meaning.

What can be inferred, and what a person decides

The line between the tool and the owner
Inferred from the SQLDecided by a person
The source table and the row filtersWhether that population is the intended one
Each aggregate, as a measureA business name for each measure
The expression that combines the measuresWhether it matches what the KPI should mean (Reach, click-to-open rate)
Measures that several KPIs shareWhich definition stands when two KPIs disagree
Whether old and new return the same valuesWhether a difference is a defect or a behaviour to keep

Three outcomes for a template

Twelve test templates: nine rewritten, three refused

Automatic

Rewritten as it stands

One aggregation over one source, grouped by the slot alone, with its parameters and optional filters kept as they are.

With a declared rule

Rewritten after a check

A join is accepted when the data shows it keeps one row per message. A row filter used by only some KPIs moves into the measure, which raises the empty-group question.

By hand

Listed and left alone

Nested aggregations, window functions, joins that multiply rows, several sources. The tool refuses them and says why.

One statement for the three KPIs · Generated and executed
WITH agg AS (
  SELECT typology, market,
    GROUPING_ID(typology, market) AS gid,
    COUNT(*) AS m1,
    COUNT(*) FILTER(WHERE C.is_delivered) AS m2,
    COUNT(DISTINCT C.client_id) FILTER(WHERE C.is_delivered) AS m3
  FROM message AS C

  HAVING COUNT(*) > 0

SELECT typology, market, gid,
  m1 AS sent,
  m2 * 1.0 / NULLIF(m1, 0) AS delivery_rate,
  m3 AS reached_clients
FROM agg

The three rewrites

  1. The grouping slot becomes grouping sets: each total is computed from the rows.
  2. Each aggregate becomes a measure; the rest is the expression. COUNT(*), used by two KPIs, is kept once.
  3. A row filter not shared by every KPI moves into its measure.

What the test showed

  • 31 legacy values compared, at detail and at every total. None differ.
  • The legacy template cannot express a grand total: an empty column list is a syntax error.
  • The nested template was refused, with a reason.

Watch for

  • Empty groups: a market with nothing delivered shows 0 reached clients after migration, and no row before. Decide which is right.
  • m1 to m3 are placeholders for the owner to name.

Tested

Four templates, nine synthetic messages, DuckDB, sqlglot 30.25.0.

Migration in waves, with proof at each step

Inference makes the first pass over a whole registry cheap, so the plan starts with an inventory and leaves the difficult KPIs for last. Each wave ends with evidence that a business owner can read.

Five waves

Each ends with evidence, not a date
1InventoryRun the inference over every registry template. Each KPI is sorted into automatic, rule-based or by hand, and shared measures are listed. This sizes the rest of the work.
2PilotMessage Sent and Delivery Rate. Old and new agree at every detail group, totals match an independent calculation, and the owner signs the two definitions.
3BulkThe remaining automatic and rule-based templates, in batches by source table. One comparison report per KPI, with every difference explained.
4By handNested and multi-source KPIs, one at a time, each with a written definition and its own tests.
5RetireReports switch to the published definitions. Old and new run side by side for an agreed period. A template is removed only after its owner signs off.

Safeguards

What keeps a migrated KPI trustworthy
Detail first. Compare old and new at detail level, where the legacy value is the reference.
Totals against an independent calculation. The legacy path cannot produce them, so it cannot be their reference.
Same data on both sides. One data version, the same filters and the same access rights for every comparison.
One rule for empty groups, decided once and applied to every KPI.
The template stays until sign-off. Rolling back means pointing the report at it again.
One definition per KPI. After sign-off the template is retired, so two sources cannot drift apart.

What inference cannot do

It reads what the SQL does, not what the KPI should mean. A join that counts a message twice is carried over faithfully, and Reach or click-to-open rate are no clearer after parsing than before. Inference shortens the migration. The owner's decision is still what closes it.

What the business decides, and the order of work

Decisions the business owns

No tool can make these
  • Reach. Messages or people? The readings give different totals: on the 13 synthetic messages, 10 were delivered to 6 distinct clients. A quick test: compare a quarter with the sum of its months. Equal suggests messages; lower means people.
  • Click-to-open rate. Clicks ÷ opens, or clicks ÷ delivered? Confirm the name and the denominator.
  • Unknown values. Whether a message from a client missing in the dimension appears as "unknown" or is dropped.
  • Exact or estimated audiences. Exact by default. An estimate is faster and must be labelled as one.
  • Which totals to show, for example market across all years, and whether a subtotal with a single line beneath it is displayed.

Order of work

Each step ends with evidence, not a date
1Agree the definitions and choose the optionEach KPI written as owner, population, numerator and denominator, and signed. The same acceptance tests run on the candidate options.
2Build the smallest working coreSent and Delivery rate match an independent check at every detail cell and total.
